# Claims check: Critic: Provocateur (Debord), round 1

Anchor used: The Society of the Spectacle (Debord), thesis 10 ("everything that appears is good; whatever is good will appear") and thesis 4 (the image as a social relation). Here the image is the system of marks: a solid, dashed, dotted or double rule that looks like a provenance audit while nothing in the repository has been audited.

No web access. Every judgement below is from memory, with confidence stated. Do not take my confidence as verification either.

## Verdict (OBJECT, no BLOCKER; four MAJOR)

The biography and the 28-work list are in good shape. I found no wrong birth year, degree year or first-publication year. The honesty apparatus is better than most fan sites: unchecked facts, unchecked years, and the unverified page numbers on the two "Source named" quotes are all disclosed. The spectacle risk is concentrated in the quotation system. Its four tiers look like the output of an investigation, but the page's own data says no source was consulted: the years are "from the owner's knowledge", the facts are "from general knowledge", and the page numbers are "not yet checked". The notes still say "We could not place this in his books", which implies a search. The line "so you can quote him without quoting someone else" promises an outcome the page cannot guarantee. One "attributed" quote (the same-rules/racist one) should be demoted, and one or two others should carry the unchecked flag more loudly. A tier whose rule is a disjunction ("credited to someone else, OR not found in his writing") has the same evidence as "Doubtful", so its separate placement is not earned.

## Findings

**1. MAJOR. The page promises a result it cannot back.**
- Page says (quotes.html): "Each one here carries a mark for how well its source is known, so you can quote him without quoting someone else."
- Problem: 10 of 20 are "attributed" with the source unchecked, and 6 are "doubtful". A reader quoting an "attributed" item is exactly as exposed as before. "Source named" still has no verified page, edition or wording.
- Confidence: high.
- Replace with: "Each one carries a mark for how well its source is known. None has yet been checked against a printed page, so treat every mark as a lead, not a licence to quote."

**2. MAJOR. "We could not place it" implies a search that the data says was never done.**
- Page says: Doubtful blurb "We could not place it"; notes "We could not place this in his books" (6 items); misattributed notes "We have not found it in Sowell's writing."
- Problem: works.json says the years are "from the owner's knowledge" and about.html says facts are "from general knowledge". No concordance, full text or index was consulted. "Could not place" reads as negative evidence. The accurate state is "no source known to the site".
- Confidence: high on the inference from the page's own disclosures. I cannot know what the owner did off-page.
- Replace the tier blurb with: "No source known to us. Nobody has yet searched his books for it. Treat it as unverified." Replace the per-quote note with: "No source known; not yet searched for." For the two misattributed: "Not known to be his; see the origin below" (see finding 5).

**3. MAJOR. The tier rules contradict each other.**
- Page says (corrections.html): "Probably not his or Doubtful becomes Widely attributed only with a printed source in his words. Widely attributed becomes Source named with a book and page."
- Problem: a printed source in his words IS a source named. Under the first rule, a doubtful quote proved by a book would stop at "attributed". The second rule makes the book-and-page step a separate promotion, which means "Source named" needs a page, but the tier's own blurb says the page is still to be checked. Tier 1 therefore asserts its own unmet condition, and the solid rule lends it audit-grade authority it has not earned.
- Confidence: high (it is a logical inconsistency).
- Replace with: "Any tier becomes Source named when a book, edition and page, or a dated original, shows the words. 'Widely attributed' means popular, not checked. We never promote a quote because it is popular." Rename tier 1 "Source claimed, unchecked" until one page has actually been verified, or add "(unchecked)" to the mark.

**4. MAJOR. One "attributed" quote should be "doubtful": the same-rules/racist line.**
- Page says: "If you have always believed that everyone should play by the same rules ... a radical 60 years ago, a liberal 30 years ago, and a racist today." Tier: attributed.
- Problem: the time spans vary between printings (I recall 50/30, 60/30 and other variants), and I recall it listed among unsourced or disputed items rather than traceable to a book or column. Variable numbers are the signature of a circulating paraphrase.
- Confidence: medium-low. It is plausibly his idea. I cannot place a source.
- Proposed change: tier -> doubtful. Note: "Widely printed as his, with differing time spans (50, 60 years). No source known."

**5. MINOR/MAJOR (borderline). "Freedom has cost too much blood and agony to be relinquished at the cheap price of rhetoric." is also a weak "attributed".**
- Problem: I cannot place it in his books. It reads more like rhetoric from another source and has no distinctive Sowell markers. The "attributed" rule (printed everywhere as his) is not evidenced for it by anything on the page.
- Confidence: low.
- Proposed change: tier -> doubtful unless the owner can name a printed source. If kept, add: "We could not tell where this circulated from."

**6. MINOR. The two "Source named" attributions: probably right, still unchecked.**
- "The most basic question is not what is best, but who shall decide what is best." -> A Conflict of Visions (1987): I am fairly sure (about 85%). It is a well-known line from that book's discussion of the constrained vision.
- "There are only two ways of telling the complete truth — anonymously and posthumously." -> Is Reality Optional? (1993): medium (about 65%). It is an aphorism-shaped line that might be an older saying he quotes, not coins. I recall it as in that 1993 collection.
- The page's own notes ("Edition and page not yet checked") are honest. Add to the second: "He may be quoting an older saying." Wording and em dash are also unchecked; say "wording as printed in secondary sources".

**7. MINOR. The Twain-type item: fairly stated about Twain, but nothing shows anyone credits it to Sowell.**
- Page says: "Often credited to Mark Twain; the saying has been traced to others." Home page: "2 circulate under his name and are probably not his."
- Problem: "traced to others" names no one. The usual research (Quote Investigator-type work) points to the Josh Billings line ("It ain't what a man don't know that makes him a fool, but what he does know that ain't so"). I am fairly confident (about 75%) the saying is not Sowell's. The page gives no evidence that it circulates under his name; the claim "circulate under his name" is unshown.
- Replace with: "Usually credited to Mark Twain; closer ancestors are sayings by Josh Billings and others. We know of no place where Sowell wrote it. We have not seen an attribution to him; it is here as a guard against one."

**8. MINOR. The Farmer item: fairly stated, wording could be firmer on the positive.**
- Page says: "Usually credited to Paul Farmer, the physician and anthropologist."
- It is a line strongly associated with Paul Farmer and Partners In Health (the author Tracy Kidder, in Mountains Beyond Mountains, 2003, popularised him). Confidence it is Farmer's: about 85%.
- Replace with: "Strongly associated with Paul Farmer, the physician and anthropologist (see Tracy Kidder's Mountains Beyond Mountains, 2003, though we have not checked the passage). We know of no place where Sowell wrote it." Also "Probably not his" is a stronger label than "not yet searched" allows (see finding 2), though fair for these two.

**9. MINOR. The tier named "misattributed" in the data vs "Probably not his" on the page.**
- Problem: the internal id is the stronger word. "Credited to someone else, or not found" mixes two different evidence states (positive counter-attribution, and absence). Doubtful items share the second state.
- Replace the tier rule with two cases: "Credited to someone else on record" (Twain, Farmer) versus nothing else. Keep "Probably not his" only for the first case.

**10. MINOR. Two "attributed" notes could say more.**
- "Much of the social history of the Western world over the past three decades ..." is dated by its own words. "Three decades" ties it to a past decade. Add: "Dated by its own wording; the original year is not recorded here."
- "There are no solutions, only trade-offs" and the "first lesson of economics is scarcity" line: I recall the second as from Basic Economics (medium confidence) and the first as a recurring formula in his talks and writing (medium). Both are probably correctly "attributed"; if the owner has the book, "scarcity" may qualify for "Source named: Basic Economics (2000)".

**11. MINOR. Biography: accurate, with three small imprecisions.**
- Born 1930 in Gastonia, North Carolina, grew up in Harlem, Marine Corps, Harvard A.B. 1958, Columbia A.M. 1959, Chicago Ph.D. 1968, Hoover 1980, Basic Economics 2000, National Humanities Medal 2002: all match my memory. Confidence: high on year and place; medium-high on the medal's year (some sources date ceremony and announcement differently).
- "1970s Teaches at Cornell, Amherst and UCLA": Cornell was mostly 1965-70 (a different decade); Amherst was a visiting post (about 1977); UCLA 1970-80 is the main 1970s post. Brandeis (1969-70), Rutgers, Howard are omitted. Replace with: "1960s-70s: Teaches at Cornell, Brandeis, UCLA and (as a visitor) Amherst, among other posts."
- "1950s Leaves school young, serves in the Marines, then studies": the order is right (left school at 17, drafted into the Marines about 1951, photographer, night classes at Howard before Harvard). "Leaves school" alone omits that he later studied at Howard. Replace with: "Leaves school at 17, serves in the Marine Corps, studies at Howard, then at Harvard (A.B. 1958) and Columbia (A.M. 1959)." Confidence: high.
- "The book most readers meet first" (Basic Economics): unsupported. Replace with: "a book written for the general reader".

**12. MINOR. The 28 works: titles and years pass; the selection and categories are weak in two places.**
- Titles and first-publication years checked from memory: all correct, including A Conflict of Visions (1987), Compassion Versus Guilt (1987), Is Reality Optional? (1993), Basic Economics (2000), A Personal Odyssey (2000), Intellectuals and Society (2010), Wealth, Poverty and Politics (2015). Confidence: high; the page disclaims anyway.
- Category issues: "Wealth, Poverty and Politics" (race) is a book about why groups differ in outcomes, spanning economics and culture; "Dismantling America" (essays) is a collected-essays volume on politics; "Say's Law" and "Classical Economics Reconsidered" are history of economic thought, grouped under "Economics". All defensible but contestable. Say "grouping is ours".
- Omissions that make "28 books, 1972 to 2023" look more complete than "selection" allows: Black Education: Myths and Tragedies (1972), Civil Rights: Rhetoric or Reality? (1984), Late-Talking Children (1997), The Einstein Syndrome (2001), Barbarians Inside the Gates (1999) and others. Suggest the home page say "28 selected books" and not "28 books", with the count tied to the selection. Confidence: high that these exist and are omitted.

**13. MINOR. Blurbs: the three are fair.**
- A Conflict of Visions: "two competing pictures of human nature that, he argues, sit underneath political disagreement". Fair; the "he argues" is the right hedge.
- Basic Economics: "built on scarcity, prices and incentives rather than jargon". Fair.
- Black Rednecks and White Liberals: "Essays on culture, history and the habits that outlast the circumstances that made them". Fair but soft: the title essay's thesis (that a Southern "cracker" or redneck culture shaped aspects of black American culture) is the contested part, and a neutral blurb for a new reader omits that it argues something specific. Confidence: medium. Optional: "Essays on culture and history, including the title essay's argument about the Southern origins of some American cultural habits."

**14. MINOR. "Four ideas": one summary overreaches slightly in tone.**
- Page says: the two visions are "one that sees its limits as fixed and one that sees them as something to be engineered away."
- Problem: his constrained/unconstrained distinction is about whether human nature's limits are permanent and which processes handle them (traditions, markets, systemic processes) versus wise planners. "Engineered away" is a loaded paraphrase of the unconstrained side, in the register of the constrained side's critics. Confidence: medium.
- Replace with: "one that treats human limits as lasting and works through trade-offs and accumulated social processes, and one that holds that wise design can overcome them."
- The other summaries ("Incentives, not intentions"; "judges a policy by what people actually do under it") are fair. The ones in the hero line, "He keeps asking one plain question: and then what happens?", is the owner's phrase set in the voice of a quotation. His better-attested formula is "compared to what? at what cost? what hard evidence?" (Economic Facts and Fallacies / his interviews; confidence medium). Replace with: "He keeps asking plain questions of every policy: compared to what, at what cost, and what actually happens next?"

**15. MINOR. The photo notice discloses but does not solve; the alt text asserts identity.**
- Page says: "Photographs are the ones the original repository carried; their source and licence were not recorded."
- The notice is adequate as disclosure and an honest offer to remove. It is not sufficient as rights clearance: publishing images of a named living person with no licence is a risk the notice transfers to the rights-holder. Alt text "A younger Thomas Sowell speaking on a television panel" asserts a date and identity nobody recorded. Confidence: medium-high.
- Replace alt with: "Photograph captioned as Thomas Sowell, date and source unknown." Consider removing the photographs until the source is found.

**16. MINOR. "Where to start" and "The question under all of it".**
- "The question under all of it" crowns one line from one book as the key to everything. That is the editor's thesis, shown in the voice of the source. Replace the heading with "One line that gets quoted often", keep the "Source named" mark, and add "(unchecked)".
- "Where to start": three picks with no stated basis. Add: "Our picks; no ranking, no survey of what readers meet first."

**17. Sufficiency of honesty claims.**
- "A selection, not a complete bibliography": sufficient on works.html and the home page, but "28 books" in the headline is stated as a count with no word "selected". See finding 12.
- "Facts written from general knowledge, not yet checked": present only on about.html. The home page's "Who he is" paragraph repeats those facts with no such flag. Add one line there.
- "Not affiliated": sufficient. "Site by SolutionsAsService, MIT licence": the MIT licence covers the code; the photographs and any quotation text are not MIT-licensable by the site, and the footer does not say so.

## Spectacle risk (where authority is performed, not earned)

- The four rule styles (solid, dashed, dotted, double) read as an evidence hierarchy. The top one is unverified. Visual weight (solid) should wait for a verified page.
- Counts as authority: "2 of the 20 quotes on this site name a book" is exact and honest, but the number does not say that none of the 2 has been verified.
- The word "Corrections", as a page title and nav item, signals an editorial process that has not yet produced a correction.
- The shape-coded 28-square stack on the home page prints a complete-looking census of a body of work that the page itself calls a selection.
- Photograph with name and a tweed jacket: portrait-as-credential.
