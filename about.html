A short life, a long shelf

The facts below are written from general knowledge and have not yet been checked line by line against sources. See Corrections.

Life

  1. 1930

    Born in Gastonia, North Carolina; grows up in Harlem, New York.

  2. 1950s

    Leaves school at 17, serves in the U.S. Marine Corps, studies at Howard, then at Harvard (A.B. 1958) and Columbia (A.M. 1959).

  3. 1968

    Ph.D. in economics, University of Chicago.

  4. 1960s–70s

    Teaches at Cornell, Brandeis, UCLA and, as a visitor, Amherst, among other posts; publishes Say's Law (1972) and Race and Economics (1975).

  5. 1980

    Joins the Hoover Institution, Stanford University, as a senior fellow.

  6. 2000

    Basic Economics, a book written for the general reader.

  7. 2002

    Awarded the National Humanities Medal.

Four ideas he returns to

  • There are no solutions, only trade-offs.

    Everything costs something. A good question about any policy is what it costs, who pays, and what else that money or time could have done.

  • Incentives, not intentions.

    He judges a policy by what people actually do under it, not by what its authors hoped they would do.

  • Two visions.

    In A Conflict of Visions he argues that many political fights come from two different pictures of human nature, one that treats human limits as lasting and works through trade-offs and accumulated social processes, and one that holds that wise design can overcome them.

  • Look at the evidence across places and times.

    His books on race, culture and migration compare many countries and centuries before they draw a conclusion.

These are summaries in our words, not quotations. Where a line is his, it is on the quotes page with its sourcing.
